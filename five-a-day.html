Five a day, every day: what the evidence says about adaptive teaching

3 min · print for the staffroom
Companion to Lab 05
1

Explicit instruction

Evidence. Rosenshine: small steps, model the thinking aloud, check for understanding before guided practice, then release. Novices learn more from worked examples than from discovery.
Tomorrow. Explain → model → check → guide → release. Put the check where it decides who you guide next.

2

Cognitive and metacognitive strategies

Evidence. Working memory is small (Sweller). Teaching pupils to plan, monitor and evaluate adds around seven months’ progress (EEF, 2018), most for lower-attaining pupils.
Tomorrow. Three problems per page, not twelve. One planning prompt before the task; one evaluating prompt after.

3

Scaffolding

Evidence. Wood, Bruner and Ross: temporary support withdrawn as competence grows. The EEF’s TA guidance orders help least to most — self-scaffold, prompt, clue, model, correct.
Tomorrow. Decide the fade when you add the scaffold. A scaffold with no exit date becomes a ceiling.

4

Flexible grouping

Evidence. Fixed setting and streaming tend to hold back lower-attaining pupils (EEF Toolkit). Temporary groups formed around today’s difficulty do not carry that cost.
Tomorrow. Group by the check, not the register. Dissolve the group when the barrier is gone.

5

Using technology

Evidence. Technology helps when it serves a clear teaching purpose — modelling, practice, feedback — and adds little when it only changes the medium (EEF, 2019).
Tomorrow. Visualiser for live modelling; speech-to-text so the pupil still does the thinking and the editing.

NOTICEDescribe the moment learning breaks down, not the diagnosis.
ADAPTChange the route in — the modelling, the chunking, the tool.
KEEPSame text, same problems, same standard. Never the destination.

Sources.
